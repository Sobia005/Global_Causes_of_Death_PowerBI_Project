# Presentation Talking Points (6–8 minutes)

## Introduction
- Over 50 million people die every year. *What* they die from depends on *where* they live.
- Data: IHME Global Burden of Disease (204 countries, 31 causes, 1990–2019) + World Bank indicators.
- Star-schema model in Power BI; causes grouped into Communicable, Non-Communicable and Injuries.
- Deaths per 100,000 people is used to compare countries of different sizes fairly.

## Page 1: Overview
- 2019: about 54M deaths, 699 per 100k, life expectancy 72.4, health spending $1,146 per person.
- Heart disease is #1 (about 18.6M, 1 in 3 deaths); cancer is #2 (about 10M).
- Trend: chronic diseases rising, infections falling.
- Demo: click a country on the map to update the whole page.

## Page 2: Causes Deep-Dive
- Deaths up 26% (42.9M → 54.2M), driven by a growing and ageing population.
- 77% non-communicable, 17% communicable, 5% injuries.
- Fastest growing: Alzheimer's (+190%), Parkinson's, kidney disease, diabetes.
- Falling: malaria, lower respiratory infections, diarrheal diseases.
- Demo: Region = Sub-Saharan Africa, where the communicable share grows sharply.

## Page 3: Country vs World
- Pakistan: 608 vs world 699 (young population), but neonatal disorders are about 18% of deaths vs about 3% globally.
- Ukraine: about double the world death rate; heart disease is over 60% of deaths.
- Heatmap: Sub-Saharan Africa has the highest infectious rate (about 357); Europe & Central Asia has the highest chronic rate (about 920).

## Page 4: Health & Wealth
- More health spending is linked to longer life ($10 → $10,000 per person; about 55 → 80+ years).
- Low clean-water access is linked to high infectious deaths.
- Low income: about 49% infections; high income: 91% chronic diseases.

## Conclusion
- Heart disease and cancer are the biggest killers.
- The world has shifted from infections to chronic diseases.
- Many deaths in poorer countries are preventable through clean water, vaccines and basic healthcare.

## Limitations
- Data ends in 2019 (no COVID-19); 31 causes only; crude (not age-adjusted) rates; model-based estimates; correlation is not causation.
