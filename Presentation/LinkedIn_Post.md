🌍 What do people around the world actually die from, and why does it depend so much on where they live?

I built an interactive Power BI dashboard to find out, using 30 years of data (1990–2019) covering 204 countries and 31 causes of death.

Key findings:
🔴 Heart disease is the world's #1 killer: about 1 in 3 deaths (18.6M in 2019)
📈 Alzheimer's deaths grew about 190% since 1990, along with diabetes and kidney disease
📉 Infectious diseases like malaria and diarrhea are falling, thanks to vaccines and clean water
🇺🇦 Ukraine's death rate (1,565 per 100k) is more than double the world's (699)
💧 Countries with low clean-water access have far higher infectious-disease deaths
💰 In low-income countries about 49% of deaths are from infections; in high-income countries 91% are from chronic diseases

The big story: the world is shifting from dying of infections to dying of chronic diseases, but many deaths in poorer countries are still preventable.

🛠️ What I used:
• Power Query to clean and unpivot the data
• A star-schema data model (fact + dimension tables)
• DAX measures for death rates, % share, 1990 vs 2019 change and country-vs-world comparisons
• Maps, scatter charts, heatmaps and cross-filtering interactions

📊 Data: IHME Global Burden of Disease (via Kaggle) + World Bank indicators
